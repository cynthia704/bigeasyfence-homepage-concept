Styles

Types of Wrought Iron Fences

Styles vary in cost, design, and durability. These are the most common.

01

Standard Fence

The most basic style and the least expensive. Straight pieces of metal are joined together to form the fence.

02

Arched Fence

More decorative than a standard fence and a bit more expensive. Curved metal pieces form an arch.

03

Spiked Fence

Built with security in mind. Sharp spikes along the top deter intruders, and it costs more than the standard and arched styles.

04

Ornamental Fence

Similar to an arched fence, with more pieces in the design and more area covered.

05

Custom Fence

A design that includes exactly the elements you want. Custom fences cost the most.

How It Works

How a Wrought Iron Fence Gets Installed

Iron installation is technical work, so a trained crew handles it. Here is what happens on your property.

5 Key StepsFrom layout to gate

Measure and Layout

Your property is measured so the fence fits precisely. If both the front yard and backyard get a fence, both are measured.

Height and Style

Fence height is chosen up front. Many homeowners go with 4 to 5 feet, and taller fences of 6 to 8 feet are an option.

Footings

Holes are dug and footings are poured to hold the fence posts.

Fabrication

The iron is cut, shaped, drilled, and formed into its final shape before it goes up at your home or business.

Gate Planned Early

If the fence includes a gate, it is planned in from the start of the project.

Benefits

Why Homeowners Choose Wrought Iron

Security and easy upkeep top the list.

2 Key PointsWhat to expect
01

Hard to Break Through

The weight and thickness of iron make it very resistant to vandalism. Kicking in a gate or climbing the fence is a lot harder than it is with lighter materials, which helps prevent theft and trespassing.

02

Easy to Clean and Protect

Smooth iron wipes clean, so dirt and grime do not build up the way they can on wood or chain link. Powder coating adds extra protection against rust, which helps most where salty air is a factor. Styles and colors range from classic black to many others.

Care and Upkeep

Caring for Your Wrought Iron Fence

Iron lasts a long time with a little attention.

01

Inspect Regularly

Look for rust or flaking metal, and for small dents or holes that let water in.

02

Fix Problems Early

A small repair now can spare you from replacing the whole fence later. Older fences often have rust spots that weaken under wind and rain.

03

Sturdy Support Underneath

A new fence needs solid footings and proper installation to stay in good shape.

04

Durable Exterior Paint

If the fence needs paint, choose a durable exterior paint and repaint on a regular schedule to protect the metal.

Donaldsonville Wrought Iron Fence Questions, Answered

What Donaldsonville homeowners ask before choosing iron.

6 Common QuestionsStraight answers
What Types of Wrought Iron Fences Are Available?
Standard, arched, spiked, ornamental, and custom. Standard is the simplest and least expensive, spiked adds a security look, and custom includes exactly the elements you ask for and costs the most.
How Is a Wrought Iron Fence Installed in Donaldsonville?
The crew measures your property, sets the height and style, digs and pours footings for the posts, and fabricates the iron before it goes up. A gate, if you want one, is planned in from the start.
Are Wrought Iron Fences Secure?
Yes. Their weight and thickness make them hard to break through, kick down, or climb, which is why many Donaldsonville homeowners pick iron to protect their property.
How Tall Can a Wrought Iron Fence Be?
Heights vary. Some homeowners choose 4 to 5 feet, others go to 6 or 8 feet, and medium heights of 5 or 6 feet are common too.
Does a Wrought Iron Fence Need Maintenance?
A little. Inspect it for rust, flaking, dents, or small holes, and fix problems early. Powder coating adds protection against rust, and durable exterior paint helps if repainting is needed.
Do You Repair Wrought Iron Fences in Donaldsonville?
Yes. Big Easy Fence handles both installation and repair of iron fences, including replacing damaged sections and dealing with rust before it spreads.
