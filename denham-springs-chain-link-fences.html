Types

Chain Link Fence Types We Install in Denham Springs

Five choices shape the fence you end up with, and the purpose of the fence guides most of them.

01

Wire Gauge

Gauge is the thickness of the wire, and a lower number means thicker wire. Tennis and basketball courts call for 9 to 11 gauge, while lighter uses like pet yards can go thinner. The job sets the choice.

02

Mesh Size

Mesh size is the width of each diamond, and a smaller diamond means more steel in the fabric. Commercial fences often use 2-inch mesh to make climbing and cutting harder around warehouses and lots.

03

Galvanized Core Wire

Zinc-coated steel is the most economical way to get a fence that resists rust and corrosion. It keeps the fence looking neat for years.

04

Vinyl Over Core Wire

A vinyl layer over the finished wire adds color and protection. It resists cracking, peeling, and tearing.

05

Polyester and PVC Colors

Rubberized PVC coatings come in natural, earthy colors, so the fence can blend into your yard or lot.

Posts and Height

Posts, Slopes, and Fence Height

Two decisions decide how well a chain link fence stands up over the years.

2 Key PointsWhat to expect
01

Posts Chosen for the Site

Posts are set into the ground, and the type is matched to your property and the job. A lighter T-post can work for a garden. Rises and dips in the ground are accounted for when the posts go in, so the line stays clean on a sloped lot.

02

Standard Heights

The most common heights are 36, 42, 48, 60, and 72 inches. Whatever height you pick, the fence is built to the same standard. Local rules can apply to fence height and placement, so confirm what your lot allows before you build.

How It Works

How Chain Link Installation Goes in Denham Springs

Chain link goes up faster than most fence types, which is one reason homeowners choose it. Here is the sequence.

5 Install StepsFrom layout to gate

Mark the Fence Line

The fence location and your property boundaries are marked before any digging starts.

Set the Posts

Posts go in at planned positions so the fence stands solid.

Attach the Fittings

Fittings on the posts help protect the fence from easy damage.

Stretch the Mesh

The mesh is pulled tight along the posts, following the boundary of the area.

Install the Gate

The gate and other accessories go in at their planned locations to finish the job.

Denham Springs Chain Link Fence Questions, Answered

What Denham Springs owners ask before choosing chain link.

6 Common QuestionsStraight answers
How Much Does a Chain Link Fence Cost in Denham Springs?
Our Denham Springs page lists $5 to $20 per linear foot for materials, up to $40 for tall, thick-gauge fence, and $10 to $20 per linear foot for labor. The average residential job is $2,193. Send us your design and materials and we will give you an honest quote.
What Wire Gauge Do I Need?
It depends on the job. Tennis and basketball courts use 9 to 11 gauge, and lighter uses like pet yards can go thinner. Remember that a lower gauge number means thicker wire. For animals that jump, a taller fence is the better call.
Which Coating Should I Choose for My Fence?
Galvanized zinc coating is the most economical and resists rust and corrosion. Vinyl-coated fence adds color and holds up against cracking and peeling. Polyester and PVC coatings offer natural colors that blend with your surroundings.
What Heights Are Available for Chain Link?
The common heights are 36, 42, 48, 60, and 72 inches. Check the local rules on fence height before you decide. Tell us the purpose of your Denham Springs fence and we can help you pick the right one.
Why Is Chain Link Easier to Install Than Other Fences?
The mesh is stretched between posts, so there are fewer parts to build than with a board or panel fence. Homeowners in Denham Springs often choose it for that reason. The sequence is simple: mark the boundaries, set the posts, add fittings, stretch the mesh, and finish with the gate.
Can You Fence a Sloped Property?
Yes. Rises and falls in the ground are taken into account when the posts are placed, so the fence follows the land. Mention the slope when you request a quote for your Denham Springs property.
