How to Choose the Right Type of Fence for Your Home

Quick Summary
  • Start with what the fence needs to do: add privacy, contain pets or kids, improve security, or simply finish the look of the yard.
  • Climate matters in Louisiana. Heavy rain, humidity, heat, and hurricane winds all affect which material and design will last.
  • Check property lines, city or parish rules, and any homeowners association limits before you settle on a design.
  • Compare materials side by side, and count upkeep as part of the cost, not just the installation price.

A fence can keep people out, keep pets and kids in, block a view, or simply finish off a yard. Whatever it is meant to do, it also has to look good with the house, since it frames the property from the street. Choosing well means sorting through a lot of options before you ever see a quote.

This guide from Big Easy Fence walks through the questions worth answering first, the things to check before building, and how the common fence materials compare. Whether you are leaning toward a classic wood picket fence or a sleek metal fence, the process is the same.

Six Questions to Answer First

What Is Your Home's Style?

A traditional house often looks right with a white picket fence or classic wood. A contemporary home may call for clean lines in metal or horizontal boards. Some designs fit both, so look at the houses on your street and at your own roofline, siding, and trim before picking a material and color.

What Is the Fence For?

Write down the main job. Privacy calls for a tall, solid fence. Keeping a dog in calls for the right height and a secure gate. Keeping people out calls for something hard to climb. A pool fence has its own safety needs. These are very different jobs, and each one points to a different fence.

What Will the Climate Do to It?

In Louisiana, heavy rain, humidity, heat, and hurricane winds are the main tests. Wet weather is hard on unprotected wood and on metal without a good coating. Strong winds are hard on tall solid fences with weak posts. Match the material to the weather your fence will actually face.

How Big Is the Yard?

A large yard has more fence line to build and maintain, which raises the cost, and it may need a taller fence to feel private. A small yard can often get by with lower fencing and fewer materials. Slopes, trees, and odd corners also change how a fence has to be built.

What Is the Budget?

Get a sense of what you are willing to spend before you start shopping, and count more than the installation. Some materials need staining or sealing every few years, while others need almost nothing. A cheaper fence that needs regular upkeep can cost more over time than a pricier one that does not.

Which Material Suits You?

Fences are made from wood, vinyl, aluminum, steel, wrought iron, and chain link, among others. Each has benefits and drawbacks, and the comparison further down covers them.

Things to Know Before Building

Consider Mixing Fence Types

You do not have to copy your neighbor. Using more than one type can add character and may save money. A wood fence across the front for looks and a chain link fence around the back, for example, puts the budget where it shows. For more ideas on cutting costs, see ways to save money on a fence.

Do Your Research

Talk to a local fence contractor and get a rough idea of what different materials cost in your area. Check the rules too. Cities and parishes often set limits on fence height and on how a fence can face the street, and they may require a permit.

Get a Quote

A written estimate tells you what you are really looking at and gives you room to adjust the budget. Ask what the quote includes, such as removal of an old fence, gates, and hardware, so nothing surprises you later.

Confirm the Property Line

Before any digging, confirm exactly where your property line runs. A survey or your property plat is the best source. If you belong to a homeowners association, check its rules on fences. Getting this right also avoids disputes with neighbors, especially if they build a fence of their own later.

Wood Fence Styles

Wood is the most familiar choice, and it comes in several styles.

  • Picket. The classic American fence, with evenly spaced pickets. It is attractive but offers little privacy.
  • Post and rail. A rustic look that suits large lots and property boundaries.
  • Lattice. A decorative panel that adds some privacy while letting light through.
  • Vertical board. Closely fitted upright boards that block views and cut down on wind and noise.
  • Composite. A mix of wood fibers and plastic that needs little upkeep but costs more than plain wood.

Metal Fences

Aluminum, steel, and wrought iron are strong and long-lasting. Aluminum resists rust, while steel and wrought iron are stronger but need their coatings maintained. All three are open by design, so add slats, panels, or plants if you want privacy. Chain link sits at the practical end of the range, and chain link fences can be customized in height and finish to suit a budget-minded plan.

Bringing It All Together

There is no one-size-fits-all answer. The right fence is the one that matches your purpose, holds up to Louisiana weather, follows local rules, and fits the way you want the yard to look. If you are still weighing choices, our fence types page lays out the options in one place.
