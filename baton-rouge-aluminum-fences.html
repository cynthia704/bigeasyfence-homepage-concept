What Makes Aluminum a Strong Fit for Baton Rouge

Aluminum is the material to look at when you want a good-looking fence with little upkeep.

5 Key BenefitsWhat aluminum delivers

No Rust

Aluminum contains no iron, so it does not rust the way steel and iron can. That suits a humid climate like Baton Rouge's.

Weather Tolerance

It handles hot summers and heavy rain, and it will not warp or rot the way wood can.

Strong for Its Weight

It is light yet sturdy enough for residential and commercial fencing.

Open Views

You can see in and out, which suits homes with a view and businesses that want an inviting front.

Style Range

Modern, classic, and eclectic properties can all be matched with the right design and finish.

Good to Know

Baton Rouge Aluminum Fence Questions, Answered

What Baton Rouge property owners ask about aluminum fencing.

6 Common QuestionsStraight answers
Does Aluminum Rust in Baton Rouge Weather?
Aluminum contains no iron, so it does not rust like steel or iron. That makes it a common pick for humid climates like Baton Rouge's. The finish still benefits from an occasional rinse and a look for chips.
Is Aluminum Strong Enough for Security?
It forms a firm barrier for homes and businesses, but it is lighter than steel. If maximum strength is the priority, compare the two during your consultation.
Can I See Through an Aluminum Fence?
Yes. Aluminum fences are open, so you can see through them. That suits homes with a view and businesses that want an inviting look.
What Custom Options Are Available for Baton Rouge Aluminum Fences?
Height, design, and finishes can be customized. You can also add decorative finials, personalized monograms, and automated gates.
How Much Maintenance Does Aluminum Need?
Occasional inspections, cleaning, and minor touch-ups. Guidance on proper care is available so the fence keeps its look.
Can I Get Privacy With Aluminum in Baton Rouge?
Aluminum is open by default. Slats or panels can be added for privacy, and it helps to raise the idea early in the design conversation.
