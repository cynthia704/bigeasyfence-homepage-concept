Choosing Wood

What to Weigh When Choosing Wood in Baton Rouge

Five questions help narrow the field before you compare prices.

5 Key FactorsWhat to weigh

Form

Match the fence to the house and the landscaping. A split-rail fence sits well with a ranch-style home, and a picket fence suits a cottage-style one.

Function

Decide the fence's job first: security, keeping animals in, or privacy from sight and noise. Different woods and styles serve each one.

Rot Resistance

A fence sits outside in all weather, so pick wood that resists rot. Cedar and pressure-treated lumber are known for it.

Specialized Lumber

Some styles call for particular products. Privacy lattice, for example, holds up best in pressure-treated lumber made for the job.

Grade

Lumber comes in construction, select, premium, and clear grades. Higher grades give a more uniform look and better quality.

Materials

Wood Options for Baton Rouge Fences

Softwoods are the usual choice. Here is how the common ones compare.

01

Cedar

Natural oils deter insects, and it balances cost with rot resistance well. It warps very little, and a finish keeps it from graying.

02

Cypress

A natural insect-deterring compound gives it strong rot resistance. It can cost more, and a finish helps it last even longer.

03

Redwood

A premium wood with a deep red color and strong resistance to insects and rot. A finish holds the color and stops graying.

04

Pressure-Treated Pine

An affordable choice. Pressure treatment adds rot and moisture resistance, so it works across many fence styles.

05

Fir

Less rot-resistant than other softwoods, yet stable and sap-free in clear, vertical-grain cuts. A reasonable pick for picket fences.

06

Spruce

Affordable and durable. It suits prefabricated, stockade-style, and picket fences, though it can warp in damp weather and attracts insects more easily.

Treatment and Finish

Treated Lumber and Finishes Explained

What is done to the wood matters as much as the species.

2 Key PointsWhat to expect
01

Pressure-Treated Lumber

Treatment forces preservatives into the wood to resist rot and insects. Kiln-dried treated lumber is less likely to warp, which is why it is the recommended choice for fencing.

02

Why a Finish Matters

A stain or sealer keeps cedar and redwood from turning gray, and it can add years to cypress. Plan on refreshing the finish as the fence ages.

Styles

Wood Fence Styles to Consider

Six wood styles come up most often.

01

Picket

Short vertical boards with gaps between them. Traditional and friendly, and a natural fit for a cottage-style home.

02

Post and Rail

Horizontal rails between posts. Open and rural in feel, and good for boundaries and ranch-style homes.

03

Lattice

A crisscross pattern that filters the view. Privacy-focused lattice holds up best in pressure-treated lumber.

04

Louver

Angled slats that block direct views while letting air move through. A modern look with some privacy built in.

05

Vertical Board

Solid, upright boards that give the most privacy of the six.

06

Ornamental

Decorative details such as shaped tops or scrollwork that turn the fence into a design feature.

Good to Know

Baton Rouge Wood Fence Questions, Answered

What Baton Rouge homeowners ask before choosing wood.

6 Common QuestionsStraight answers
What Is the Best Wood for a Fence in Baton Rouge?
Cedar, cypress, and redwood are the top options because each resists insects and rot. Cedar balances cost and durability, cypress can cost more, and redwood is a premium choice. Pressure-treated pine is the budget-friendly pick.
Is Pressure-Treated Wood a Good Fence Choice?
Yes, for many fences. It resists rot and insects, and kiln-dried treated lumber is less likely to warp. It also suits styles like lattice, where extra durability helps.
How Do I Match a Fence to My Baton Rouge Home?
Look at the house style and the landscaping first. Split rail goes well with a ranch-style home and pickets with a cottage-style one. Then decide how much privacy you need.
What Does Lumber Grade Mean?
Grade describes the quality of the boards. Construction, select, premium, and clear are the grades you will see, with higher grades giving a more uniform look and better quality.
Can a Wood Fence Handle Baton Rouge Weather?
Rot-resistant wood, pressure-treated lumber, and a good finish all help a fence hold up outdoors. Damp conditions are hard on any wood, so choose materials with that in mind.
