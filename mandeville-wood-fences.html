Before You Choose

Five Things to Weigh Before Choosing Wood

Picking the right wood comes down to a few practical questions.

01

Form

Match the fence to your home and landscape. Ranch-style homes often suit split-rail wood, while cottage-style homes tend to pair well with pickets.

02

Function

Decide what the fence is for. Security, keeping animals in, visual privacy, and noise reduction each point to a different design.

03

Rot Resistance

A fence sits outdoors year-round, so rot resistance matters. Cedar and pressure-treated lumber are popular for exactly that reason.

04

Specialized Lumber

Some lumber products are treated for added durability. A privacy screen, for example, does well with lumber matched to that job.

05

Grade

Lumber comes in construction, select, premium, and clear grades. Higher grades give a more uniform look and better overall quality.

Top Picks

Top Wood Choices for Mandeville Fences

Three woods come up most often when homeowners want a fence that lasts.

3 Key PointsWhat to expect
01

Cedar

The natural oils in cedar deter insects, and its rot resistance helps the fence last longer. It strikes a good balance between price and longevity.

02

Cypress

Cypress resists rot and contains a natural insect deterrent called cypretine. It is durable and sits toward the higher end on price.

03

Redwood

Known for its deep red color and used widely for fencing. It costs more, but it delivers excellent quality and durability. Stain or treat it to keep it from graying.

More Options

Treated Lumber and Other Wood Options

Most wood fences are built from softwood cut from evergreen trees. These are the other choices to know.

01

Pressure-Treated Wood

Chemically treated to resist rot and insects. Kiln-dried pressure-treated boards are the recommended choice because they warp less.

02

Pine

A popular, affordable pick. Pressure treatment adds durability and moisture resistance, and pine works well for picket and board fences.

03

Fir

Less rot-resistant than some softwoods, but stable and sap-free in clear, vertical-grain sections. That makes it a fit for picket fences.

04

Spruce

A cost-effective option for prefabricated, stockade-style, or picket fences. It can warp in damp conditions and is more prone to insect damage.

05

White Oak

Known for its strength and used on ranches and horse farms. It can warp or bow over time.

Mandeville Wood Fence Questions, Answered

What Mandeville property owners ask before choosing wood.

6 Common QuestionsStraight answers
What Are the Advantages of a Wood Fence in Mandeville?
Wood has a natural, classic look and is customizable and versatile in design. It offers privacy and security, and it is cost-effective, easy to repair, and easy to modify later.
How Do I Maintain a Wood Fence?
Stain or paint it, seal it, clean it, and fix damage promptly. Regular care helps a Mandeville fence resist rot, insect problems, and warping, which extends its life.
Can a Wood Fence Be Customized?
Yes. You choose the fence height, style, and stain or paint color, and you can add features like lattice or post caps. That lets a Mandeville fence match the look of your property.
Is Wood an Eco-Friendly Fence Choice?
Wood is a renewable, sustainable resource, which suits Mandeville homeowners who care about the environment. Responsible sourcing and manufacturing make it a good fit for homeowners with sustainability goals.
Should I Hire a Contractor to Build My Wood Fence?
Building a wood fence takes the right tools, skills, and knowledge. A professional contractor builds a sturdy, well-constructed fence and handles the work from start to finish.
Which Lumber Grade Should I Ask About?
Wood is sold in construction, select, premium, and clear grades. Higher grades give a more uniform look, better durability, and higher quality, so ask which grade fits your fence and budget.
