Repair or Replace

Repair or Replace Your Walker Fence

Repair usually costs less than replacement, until the damage passes a certain point.

3 Key PointsWhat to expect
01

Repair When Damage Is Under 20%

A rule of thumb from our Walker page: when less than 20 percent of the fence is damaged, repair is the smarter move. Typical fixes include cutting out and patching rot, swapping in new nails, and bracing a sagging section.

02

Replace When Damage Passes 20%

If more than 20 percent of the panels need replacing, it is time for a new fence. A fence that is rotten all the way through is best replaced, so the rot does not spread to sound sections.

03

Small Problems Grow

Fixing minor issues early prevents costlier, sometimes irreparable, problems later. A repaired fence also keeps your property secure and looks better to prospective buyers if you sell.

Warning Signs

What to Look for on Wood and Iron Fences

Catch these early and the repair stays small. If you see any of them, it is time to call a pro.

01

Rot and Termite Damage

Soft or crumbling wood, or wood that is chipping away, points to rot or termites. Both weaken boards and posts.

02

Loose or Rusted Nails

Missing, popped, or rusted nails let boards work loose, and panels can go missing over time.

03

Sagging or Wobbly Posts

Posts that lean, droop, or wobble, on wood or iron, deserve a professional look before they give way.

04

Cracking or Peeling Paint

Paint that chips, cracks, or peels exposes the wood or metal underneath to moisture.

05

Rust on Iron

Rust on an iron fence shows the finish has failed somewhere, and it tends to spread.

06

Bent Rails and Gaps

Bent rails and missing sections mean the fence is no longer keeping the property secure.

Good to Know

Walker Fence Repair Questions, Answered

What Walker property owners ask before booking a repair.

6 Common QuestionsStraight answers
How Much Does Fence Repair Cost in Walker?
Our Walker page puts the average repair at $250 to $750, or $15 to $35 per foot. Small repairs to holes, cracks, wood rot, or missing boards run $100 to $200, and replacing posts and panels or fixing a leaning fence runs $250 to $1,000. Ask for a free estimate for your fence.
When Is a Fence Past Repair?
A common rule of thumb is 20 percent. If less than 20 percent of the fence is damaged, repair is usually best. If more than 20 percent of the panels need replacing, it is time to replace the fence.
What Are the Signs an Iron Fence Needs Repair?
Look for posts that have shifted and started to droop, sections that are missing, peeling paint, rust, and bent rails. Any of these is a good reason to have the fence looked at.
What Wood Fence Problems Can Be Repaired in Walker?
Rot, damage from termites, missing or rusted nails, missing panels, sagging posts, wobbly sections, and chipping or cracking paint. We repair wood, chain-link, PVC, metal, aluminum, vinyl, and wrought iron fences.
How Are Rot and Sagging Fixed?
For rot, the infected area is cut out completely with a saw and patched or replaced with new wood. Rusted or popped nails are replaced, and a sagging or wobbly fence gets support from a brace or tension rod. If a section is rotten through, it is replaced so the rot cannot spread.
Does Fence Repair Add Value to a Home in Walker?
It can help. A repaired, well-kept fence supports curb appeal and can make the property look better to prospective buyers. It also restores the security and privacy the fence was installed to provide.
