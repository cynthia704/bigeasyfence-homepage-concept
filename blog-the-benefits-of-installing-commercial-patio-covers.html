The Benefits of Installing Commercial Patio Covers: A Comprehensive Guide

Quick Summary
  • Solid, lattice, and retractable covers each solve a different problem, so the right pick depends on how your customers use the space.
  • A cover makes an outdoor area usable through Louisiana sun and rain, and lighting can stretch that use into the evening.
  • Compare bids only when each one covers the same size, materials, and finish, and ask about wind guidance for storm season.
  • Routine cleaning and a quick look at fasteners and moving parts keep a cover working and looking good for years.

A patio cover turns a hot, wet, or glary stretch of outdoor space into somewhere people actually want to sit. For a New Orleans business, that might be a restaurant patio that stays open through a summer shower or a courtyard where staff can eat lunch out of the sun. This guide covers the main types of covers, what they add, and what installation and upkeep involve.

Here is how the team at Big Easy Fence would walk a business owner through choosing a commercial patio cover, starting with the three designs you will see most often.

Three Types of Commercial Patio Covers

Most covers fall into one of three groups. Each one balances shade, rain protection, and airflow a little differently, and the trade-offs matter more than the look. If you want to compare materials as well, our post on choosing the right material for a commercial patio cover goes deeper.

Solid Covers

A solid cover has a closed roof, so it blocks nearly all sun and keeps rain off the seating area. That makes it the steadiest choice for a space that has to stay open in any weather. The trade-off is airflow. Without open sides or fans, a fully covered patio can feel close on a still, humid afternoon.

Lattice Covers

A lattice cover has evenly spaced gaps in the roof. It filters the sun instead of blocking it, which gives a patio dappled shade and lets warm air rise and escape. Lattice suits courtyards and entryways where you want light and breeze. It does little against rain, so plan on furniture that can get wet.

Retractable Covers

A retractable cover opens and closes as the weather changes. On a mild evening it stays open. During a downpour or a harsh afternoon it closes. Motorized versions make that a button press. Because they have moving parts, they need more routine care than a fixed roof.

What a Patio Cover Adds to a Business

The benefits show up in three places: how often the space gets used, how comfortable it feels, and how the property looks from the street. For more on the design side, see how commercial patio covers enhance outdoor spaces.

A Space Customers Can Use More Often

An uncovered patio is out of play on a rainy day or under a hot afternoon sun. A covered one is not. For a restaurant, cafe, or event space, that means more hours when the outdoor seating is available. For an office or retail center, it means a spot where people can wait, meet, or take a break.

Comfort in Louisiana Heat and Rain

Shade takes a real edge off a summer afternoon, and a roof keeps the pop-up showers from clearing the tables. Pair a cover with fans and you improve the comfort further. Comfort keeps people in the space longer, and staff notice it too.

Curb Appeal and Lighting

A cover can be styled to match the building, from a simple painted frame to a more detailed design. Integrated lighting extends the space into the evening and gives the entrance a finished look after dark. Ask your contractor to plan the wiring during design so the fixtures sit where you want them.

What Drives the Cost of a Patio Cover

No honest price applies to every project, so this post does not quote one. Cost depends on the size of the covered area, the type of cover, the materials, the finish, and how much site work the job needs. A solid roof generally involves more framing and roofing material than an open lattice. A motorized system adds mechanical parts and controls. Lighting and fans add their own line items.

Compare Bids on Equal Terms

When you collect quotes, ask each contractor to price the same size, the same materials, and the same finish. Otherwise the numbers are not comparable, and the cheapest bid may simply be the smallest scope.

How Installation Usually Goes

Every contractor runs the job a little differently, but the sequence tends to look like this.

Planning the Project

  1. Walk the site. The contractor looks at the space, the building, and how the area is used, then talks through what you want it to do.
  2. Choose the type and materials. You settle on solid, lattice, or retractable, and pick a frame material and finish that suit the building.
  3. Confirm the paperwork. Ask what approvals the project needs and who handles them before any work starts.

Build Day and Handoff

  1. Build and install. Crews set the posts, frame the structure, and install the roof. Leave any electrical work to qualified electricians.
  2. Walk it together. Before the job closes, look over the finished work, test any moving parts, and ask for care instructions.

Storm season deserves a direct question. Ask what wind guidance applies to your cover, and whether a retractable roof should be closed or secured before a named storm.

Keeping a Patio Cover in Good Shape

Most upkeep is light. A few habits go a long way in a humid climate.

Routine Cleaning

  • Wash it regularly. A soft brush and mild detergent clear dirt and debris. Humidity encourages mildew in shaded corners, so give those spots extra attention.
  • Keep lattice openings clear. Leaves, vines, and buildup block airflow and add weight.

Moving Parts and Storm Checks

  • Check moving parts. On a retractable cover, follow the manufacturer's care instructions for tracks and hardware, and have a qualified technician handle the motor and any electrical service.
  • Look after storms. Loose fasteners, shifted panels, and damaged finish are easier to fix early.

Is a Patio Cover Right for Your Property?

If the outdoor space around your building sits empty for half the year because of heat or rain, a cover is worth a close look. Start by noting when the space gets used, where the sun and rain hit, and how many people it needs to hold. Bring those notes to a contractor who handles commercial outdoor projects and the conversation gets much more specific.
