Why Wrought Iron

Why Choose Wrought Iron for Your Fence

A well-chosen fence adds security and counts as a real home improvement, even if you have no plans to sell.

5 Key BenefitsWhat wrought iron delivers

Durability and Longevity

With proper care, a wrought iron fence can last for decades. Unlike wood, it does not need periodic replacement.

Design Flexibility

Clean, classic lines or ornate scrollwork: iron can be shaped to fit the look of your home or business.

Strength

Iron resists breakage far better than wood, which is why it is a favorite for commercial properties that need added safety and security.

Open Views

Iron balances security with visibility. You can see through it, and rail thickness can be tailored to your preferences.

Pest Resistance

Termites and other pests that chew through wood have nothing to feed on, so there is no pest damage to repair.

The Material

Wrought Iron Versus Cast Iron

The two get lumped together, but they are different metals made in different ways.

2 Key PointsWhat to expect
01

What Wrought Iron Is

Wrought iron is an iron alloy with very little carbon, which makes it strong and easy to shape. It has been used for centuries in everything from tools and railways to weapons, and fences fit right into that history.

02

How Cast Iron Differs

Cast iron carries far more carbon, roughly 2 to 4 percent, and is poured into molds. Wrought iron is worked by heating, bending, and hammering, and that shaping is what gives it toughness. Cast iron is hard but more brittle.

Ways to Use It

Where Wrought Iron Works Best

Iron does far more than run along a property line.

01

Decorative Gates

An iron gate makes a strong first impression at a driveway or front walk, and it sets the tone for the rest of the property.

02

Garden Fences

Light, open iron panels frame a garden without hiding it, and they pair well with decorative gates.

03

Brick Walls and Pillars

Iron between brick or stone pillars is a classic combination that adds height and security to a wall.

04

Mixed-Material Fences

Iron sections can be blended with other materials, so one fence line can carry different looks from front to back.

What It Costs

What Goes Into Wrought Iron Fence Cost

Wrought iron fences are typically custom made, so the price is set by the project. A free estimate gives you your real number.

01

Custom Fabrication

Because most iron fences are built to order, the cost follows the work that goes into your particular design.

02

Design Detail

Simple straight pickets cost less than elaborate scrollwork, finials, and decorative panels.

03

Height and Strength

Taller fences and heavier bars use more material and take more labor.

04

Length of the Run

Iron is usually priced by the linear foot, so a longer fence line adds up.

05

Installation and Site Work

Setting the posts and hanging the panels takes skilled craftsmanship, and that labor is part of the total.

Before You Build

Check These Before Installation

A little homework up front avoids expensive surprises later.

01

Underground Utilities

Call 811 before any digging so buried lines get marked. Post holes should never go in blind.

02

Property Lines

Confirm exactly where your property ends so the fence sits on your side of the line.

03

HOA Guidelines

If you belong to a homeowners association, check its approved guidelines for fence style and height before you order.

Care and Upkeep

How to Maintain a Wrought Iron Fence

Iron is durable, so the upkeep is light. It just needs to be regular.

2 Key PointsWhat to expect
01

Inspect It Regularly

Walk the fence line and look for chipped paint and rust spots. Damp weather is hard on exposed metal, and New Orleans humidity gives it plenty to work with, so a quick look every so often pays off.

02

Treat Rust Early

Small rust spots can be sanded away along with the loose paint around them. An oil-based primer over the bare metal helps stop rust from forming under the new paint. For widespread rust or damaged sections, bring in a professional.

Good to Know

Wrought Iron Fence Questions, Answered

What New Orleans property owners ask before choosing iron.

6 Common QuestionsStraight answers
What Is Wrought Iron?
It is an iron alloy with very little carbon, prized for being strong and easy to shape. It has a long history in toolmaking and railways, and in fencing it is valued for both strength and detail.
How Is Wrought Iron Different From Cast Iron?
Cast iron has much more carbon and is poured into molds. Wrought iron is heated, bent, and hammered into shape, which gives it its toughness. Cast iron is hard but more brittle.
How Much Does a Wrought Iron Fence Cost in New Orleans?
It depends on the design, the height, the strength of the bars, and how long the fence line is. Most iron fences are custom made and priced by the linear foot, with installation labor on top. A free estimate is the quickest way to get a real figure for your property.
How Do I Keep a Wrought Iron Fence From Rusting?
Inspect it regularly for chipped paint and rust spots, especially in damp weather. Sand off rust and loose paint, prime with an oil-based primer, and repaint. Catching a small spot early keeps a small job small.
Can Termites or Other Pests Damage Wrought Iron?
No. Pests that eat through wood have nothing to feed on in an iron fence, so you avoid the pest damage and treatment costs that come with other materials.
What Should I Check Before Installing an Iron Fence?
Find out where underground utilities run by calling 811, confirm your property lines, and review your homeowners association guidelines if you have one. Then talk through design, height, and gates so the estimate matches what you want.
