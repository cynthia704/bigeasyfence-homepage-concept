Why Wood

Why Choose a Wood Fence

Wood keeps earning its spot around homes and businesses in New Orleans.

01

Cost-Effective

Wood usually costs less than many other fence materials, and better wood treatments have improved how well it holds up outdoors.

02

Lightweight

Boards and pickets are light to handle, which makes wood easy to shape to your yard and your design.

03

Full of Character

Wood suits a cottage, a ranch, or a modern home, and it can be finished to match your style.

Choosing Wood

What to Weigh Before Choosing Wood

Five questions shape the right fence for your property.

5 Key FactorsDecide these first

Form

Match the fence to the house and the landscape. A split-rail fence suits a ranch-style home, while a picket fence is a natural fit for a cottage.

Function

Decide the main job first. Security, keeping animals in, and privacy from sight or noise each point toward a different design.

Rot Resistance

A fence outdoors in New Orleans humidity needs wood that resists rot. Cedar and pressure-treated lumber are popular for that reason.

Specialized Lumber

Some designs call for specific products. A lattice privacy screen, for example, does best with lumber treated for extra durability.

Grade

Construction, select, premium, and clear grades are generally the safer picks. They give a more even look and better quality than lower grades.

Materials

Best Woods for a New Orleans Fence

These four come up most often.

01

Cedar

Natural oils help cedar deter insects. It balances price and lifespan, and its rot resistance helps it last.

02

Cypress

A durable option that resists rot and deters insects through its own natural oils.

03

Redwood

Prized for its deep red color and widely used for fencing. It costs more, and it can be treated or stained to keep it from turning gray.

04

Pine

A popular, affordable pick for pickets and boards. Pressure treatment improves its durability and moisture resistance.

Other Options

Pressure-Treated Lumber and Softer Woods

A few more materials round out the list.

3 Key PointsWhat to expect
01

Pressure-Treated Lumber

This lumber is chemically treated to resist rot and insects. Kiln-dried boards are worth asking about, since they are less likely to warp.

02

Fir and Spruce

Fir is stable and sap-free in clear, vertical-grain cuts, which suits picket fences, though it resists rot less well than cedar or redwood. Spruce is affordable and common in stockade and picket panels, but it can warp in damp conditions and is more prone to insects.

03

White Oak

A strong, popular choice for ranches and horse farms. It tends to warp or bow over time.

Styles

From classic pickets to modern louvers.

01

Good to Know

Wood Fence Questions, Answered

What New Orleans homeowners ask before choosing wood.

6 Common QuestionsStraight answers
Which Wood Is Best for a Fence in New Orleans?
Cedar, cypress, and redwood are the top choices. Cedar balances cost and lifespan, cypress resists rot on its own, and redwood offers rich color at a higher price. Pine, especially pressure-treated, is the budget-friendly option.
What Should I Consider Before Choosing Wood?
Start with form, function, rot resistance, specialized lumber needs, and grade. Match the style to your house, decide whether privacy or security matters most, and pick wood that can handle New Orleans humidity.
Is Pressure-Treated Wood a Good Choice?
It is chemically treated to resist rot and insects, so it is a common pick for outdoor use. Kiln-dried boards are less likely to warp. Ask your installer which treatment suits your project.
What Wood Fence Styles Are Available?
Wood picket, post and rail, lattice, louver, vertical board, and ornamental styles are the main choices. Picket suits cottages, rail suits large lots, and vertical board gives the most privacy.
Does Big Easy Fence Build Wood Fences for Businesses?
Yes. Wood fences are installed for both residential and commercial properties in New Orleans. The commercial wood fences page covers business projects.
Are Wood Fences a Good Value?
Wood is cost-effective, lightweight, and adds character to a home. Improved wood treatments have made modern wood fences more durable than they used to be, and the species you pick shifts both price and lifespan.
