5 Privacy Fences You Can Get at an Affordable Price

Quick Summary
  • Privacy does not have to be expensive: wood, metal, vinyl, hedges, and lattice-top panels all offer options for tighter budgets.
  • The five options covered are pallet wood panels, metal panels, vinyl panels, living fences, and lattice-top privacy panels.
  • Cheap only pays off when the fence also handles Louisiana heat, sun, and humidity and stays easy to maintain.
  • Wash the finish now and then and check the posts, because failing posts cause most fence problems.

For many households, privacy comes first. A well-chosen privacy fence keeps your yard to yourself, and it can also add security, curb appeal, and even some value to the property. The good news is that you do not need a huge budget to get there.

Aluminum sits at the pricey end of the range, but plenty of cheaper materials work well. Big Easy Fence helps New Orleans property owners find a fence that fits their yard and their wallet, and the lowest-cost options usually come from three groups: wood, metal, and vinyl. The trick is to balance cost against looks, durability, and how well the material suits our climate.

Five Privacy Fence Ideas for Tighter Budgets

Here are five budget-friendly ways to get real privacy, with the trade-offs of each.

1. Pallet Wood Fence Panels

Pallet panels work in almost any setting, from a city lot to open country. The design is flexible enough to complement your backyard decor, and it keeps nosy neighbors out of your business while still letting in some sunlight. Depending on where you find the pallets, the wood itself may be free.

Pallet fences are popular for DIY projects. Do a little homework first, though. Choose pallets stamped as heat-treated rather than chemically fumigated, and seal the wood well, because bare wood soaks up humidity fast. If you want a professional look to go with a carefully planned yard, a crew can build a cleaner version.

2. Metal Fence Panels

Metal fences come in a wide range of styles and materials, including aluminum, wrought iron, and solid sheet panels. Solid metal panels give the most privacy, and they are low maintenance and reasonably priced. Wrought iron panels with dense patterns can offer good privacy while still looking elegant. With so many options, there is a metal panel for almost every budget.

3. Vinyl Fence Panels

Vinyl, made of polyvinyl chloride (PVC), is a great alternative to wood. It resists UV light and solar heat, so it does not splinter or crack from year-round sun. Insects do not bother it, and it does not rot. That keeps upkeep low.

The starting price of a vinyl fence can be higher than some options, but its long life and low maintenance help balance that out. Competition among manufacturers has also brought prices down while keeping quality good. Color is a big part of the appeal, from white and tan to bolder shades and wood-grain finishes. This guide to picking the best vinyl fence color makes the choice easier.

4. Living Privacy Fences

A living fence skips the panels altogether. It is inexpensive, effective, and adds greenery to the yard. Evergreen shrubs make a natural screen that looks good all year, and once they are tall you can shape them to suit your taste.

The tradeoffs are time and care. A hedge takes a few seasons to fill in, and it needs trimming. Ask a local nursery which plants thrive in Louisiana heat and humidity, since not every popular hedge does well here.

5. Lattice-Top Privacy Panels

Lattice-top panels put a crisscross of wood or metal slats above a solid panel of metal, wood, or vinyl. You still get privacy at eye level, while sunlight and air pass through the top. It is a relaxed twist on the standard privacy panel and lets you keep some of the view outside.

Choosing the Best Fence for Privacy

There is no one-size-fits-all answer. Every household has its own needs, and some fences cost less than others, while some offer better privacy and others last longer. Talking through your priorities with a fence professional is the quickest way to land on the right style. If privacy is the main goal, our look at the strongest type of privacy fence is worth a read.

Materials for Our Climate

Louisiana summers are hot and humid, so the material should resist UV rays and moisture as a minimum. It should also be easy to maintain and touch up. Treated wood, metal, and vinyl panels all meet that standard, and the fence should work as an extension of your home's look and a screen at the same time.

What Drives the Price

The price of privacy panels depends on the strength of the material and the design of the panel. Salvaged pallets can cost nothing, while metal and vinyl panels cost more but need less care over the years. Always compare quotes for the same height and length so the numbers mean something.

Keeping Your Privacy Fence in Good Shape

A privacy fence only works if it stays in good shape. Give the finish a wash now and then to clear dirt, grime, and algae, using mild soap and water or a pressure washer on a low setting. Repainting or restaining every so often brings back the shine.

Check the structure too. Failing posts are a common cause of fence problems, so make sure the posts and supports are solid and repair anything that leans or loosens. If you need help pricing a job, a free estimate from a local fence company is a good place to begin.
